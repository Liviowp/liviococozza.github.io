Empathy Is Built Through Experience, Not Words

Why genuine connection is created through shared moments, emotions and real-life experiences.

Nowadays, there is a lot of talk about empathy: on television, on social media, and in paid courses. It is constantly emphasized how essential it is, especially in both verbal and non-verbal communication.

Listening, understanding, and knowing how to give the right answer also means putting yourself in another person’s shoes, trying to perceive the emotions they are feeling.

But what is the true outcome of empathy?

It is creating experiences—both positive and negative—because those are precisely what shape us. They are the feelings that remain in our minds, become part of our life experiences, and allow us to develop reasoning processes and motor patterns useful for facing life.

Empathy is also the ability to go beyond the rules, to not always choose the easiest, least risky, or fastest response, but to understand when it is necessary to do something different, keeping the person and their emotions at the center.

So, let’s go back to experiencing emotions outside of social media, cell phones, and television. Let’s invite someone out for a drink. Let’s have children sing on the bus. Let’s joke and laugh together after a hard workout session. Let’s create authentic moments that leave a mark and build genuine relationships.

Because empathy isn’t learned simply by listening to someone talk about it. It develops through the experiences, relationships, and emotions we share with others.

Let’s live empathy, not just talk about it.

And stop believing that reading countless books, studying for ten degrees, and attending a hundred courses will give you the keys to being the perfect person to understand the world. If inside you don’t have the right drive, a genuine smile, and hands-on experience, you will never, ever be able to put true empathy into practice.
